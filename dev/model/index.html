2. The model

2.1 Counts on a latent Gaussian layer

The model has two layers. The bottom layer is the measurement. The top layer is the biology, on a log scale where effects add up.

  • assumption — given the latent log abundance $Z_{ij}$, the counts are independent Poisson variables:

\[y_{ij} \mid Z_{ij} \;\sim\; \text{Poisson}\big(e^{\,o_i + Z_{ij}}\big),\]

where $o_i$ is a known offset, by default the log library size $\log \sum_j y_{ij}$.

  • assumption — the latent log abundances follow a regression on the design, latent factors and independent Gaussian noise:

\[Z_{ij} \;=\; x_i^\top B_j \;+\; w_i^\top \Gamma_j \;+\; \varepsilon_{ij}, \qquad \varepsilon_{ij} \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_j^2).\]

Here $x_i$ is the row of the design matrix $X$ ($n \times k$, with an intercept), $B_j$ the coefficients of gene $j$, $w_i$ the $q$ latent factors of sample $i$, $\Gamma_j$ their loadings on gene $j$, and $\sigma_j^2$ the overdispersion of gene $j$.

This is the Poisson-lognormal (PLN) model of Aitchison & Ho (1989), with a low-rank covariance as in Chiquet et al. (2018). Integrating $Z$ out, a count has mean and variance

\[\mathbb E[y_{ij}] = \lambda_{ij} = e^{o_i + \mu_{ij} + \sigma_j^2/2}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}[y_{ij}] = \lambda_{ij} + \lambda_{ij}^2\,\big(e^{\sigma_j^2} - 1\big),\]

with $\mu_{ij} = x_i^\top B_j + w_i^\top \Gamma_j$. This is the quadratic mean–variance relationship of the negative binomial. The Poisson part dominates for small counts, and the lognormal part (biological variability) dominates for large counts. Zeros need no special treatment and no pseudo-count.

Why Poisson-lognormal and not negative binomial

Both have the same mean–variance relationship. The lognormal layer has two advantages here. Effects, factors and noise all add up on the same log scale, so the latent layer is an ordinary Gaussian factor model, and every method for confounders (CATE, RUV, MOUTHWASH) applies to it. And it is closed under binomial thinning: thinning a Poisson($\lambda$) count with probability $\pi$ gives a Poisson($\pi\lambda$) count. The simulations of §6, which thin real counts, therefore stay exactly inside the model.

2.2 The confounders and the offset factor

The latent factors $w_i$ play the role of the hidden confounders of §1.5, with the part correlated with the design already absorbed in $B_j$:

\[B_j \;=\; \beta_j + \alpha^\top \Gamma_j \qquad\text{(for the covariates of interest)} .\]

So $B_j$ is the confounded effect $\tilde\beta_j$, and the factors $w_i$ are, by construction, uncorrelated with the design ($X^\top W = 0$, see §3).

  • assumption (optional, on by default) — the first factor has loadings fixed to one: $\Gamma_{1j} = 1$ for every gene.

This is the offset factor of §1.6. Its value $w_{i1}$ is a per-sample shift, common to all genes. It absorbs the difference between the true offset (with the unknown total RNA content) and the library size used in $o_i$. Its part correlated with the design is the compositional shift $\delta = \alpha_1$.

2.3 What is identified

  • result — without further information, $\beta_j$ and $\alpha$ are not separately identified: only $B_j = \beta_j + \alpha^\top\Gamma_j$ is.
  • assumption — either a set $C$ of negative control genes has $\beta_j = 0$ for the covariate of interest, or the distribution of the $\beta_j$ has a point mass at zero (sparsity).

With either assumption, $\alpha$ and therefore every $\beta_j$ are identified, including the compositional shift. The two options use the same code: a control gene is a gene whose prior is the point mass at zero (§4.2).

2.4 How it is fitted

The model is fitted by empirical Bayes: a variational EM for the PLN layer (§5), then MOUTHWASH, which estimates the confounders together with an adaptive prior on the effects and shrinks the variances across genes (§4). The confounders $\alpha$ and loadings $\Gamma$ are point estimates; the uncertainty of $\alpha$ and the misfit of the variances enter the standard errors (§4.2). There is no prior to choose, and a fit takes seconds. A fully Bayesian version, a Gibbs sampler that integrates over $\alpha$ and $\Gamma$, is kept on the fully-bayesian branch of the repository until the cause of its miscalibration on real data is understood.

Functions

  • pln: fit the PLN model with a design, latent factors and the offset factor.
  • pln_mouthwash: the complete empirical Bayes branch.
