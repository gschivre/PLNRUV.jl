7. Validation

Each component is checked against a known truth, and, where one exists, against the reference implementation in R. The R packages are used only for these checks, in the separate benchmark/ environment. The package itself is pure Julia. Unless stated otherwise, "FDP" is the false discovery proportion among the genes declared at s-value < 0.05, counting both null genes and wrong signs as errors.

Sections 7.1–7.3 check each component on simulations; section 7.4 benchmarks the whole method against standard pipelines on real counts.

7.1 The simulator

7.2 ash and MOUTHWASH

  • ash, 5 000 effects, 20 % non-null with sd 2, standard errors 0.5: $\hat\pi_0 = 0.78$ (truth 0.8 plus the near-zero effects), RMSE 0.29 against 0.50 for the raw estimates, FDP 4.8 %.
  • MOUTHWASH against vicar. With the same model (three free factors, normal likelihood, no offset factor), the two implementations agree to two or three decimals in RMSE, number of discoveries and FDP.
  • The offset factor. Simulated log abundances, 40 samples, 1 000 genes, two confounders, compositional shift of 1 correlated with the group, three replicates:

7.3 The PLN layer

Counts simulated from the PLN model: 30 samples, 1 500 genes, 150 controls, two confounders, compositional shift of 1, overdispersions $\sigma_j^2 \sim \text{Gamma}(4, 0.05)$.

  • E-step. The Lambert W solution matches a brute-force maximisation ($M = 0.50043$ against $0.50045$ on a grid of step $5\times 10^{-5}$). $W_0(e^t)$ is accurate to $3\times10^{-15}$ and finite up to $t = 800$.
  • Standard errors. With no confounding, at low depth (22 % zeros): $\hat\lambda \approx 1.0$, coverage of 95 % intervals 95–96 %, FDP 2.5–4.6 %. The latent-scale standard error of §5.4 needs no inflation.
  • Against a Gaussian analysis of log-CPM. Similar at a median count of 32. At a median count of about 3, the PLN branch has a lower RMSE (0.33–0.43 against 0.36–0.45) and a lower FDP.
  • Variance shrinkage, low depth with confounding, mean FDP over four replicates: none 7.1 %, limma 6.3 %, vash 5.4 %, at equal RMSE. On variances with a bimodal distribution, the mean squared error of $\log\tilde s_j^2$ is 0.42 raw, 0.27 with limma and 0.24 with vash.

Remaining miscalibration of the empirical Bayes branch. With confounding and low counts, the FDP is still slightly above the nominal level. It comes from the estimation of the confounders (the Gaussian pipeline shows the same excess), not from the PLN layer.

7.4 Benchmark on thinned GTEx data

Two-group experiments are simulated from GTEx v8 skeletal muscle by binomial thinning (§6): 2 000 genes, 200 negative controls, 20 samples, 10 replicates per scenario. Balanced: 20 % of the genes change, log₂ fold changes $\sim \mathcal N(0, 0.8)$, random groups. Confounded: the same effects, groups correlated with two hidden factors of the samples (0.6 and 0.2). Global increase: 60 % of the genes go up, log₂ fold changes $\sim \mathcal N(1, 0.5)$, confounded. Two oracles give a reference: PLN with the confounding factors and the true normalisation known, and the same with the true effect distribution in place of the fitted ash prior.

FDP / power at level 0.05 (s-values for PLNRUV and vicar, BH-adjusted p-values otherwise), means over 10 replicates. PLNRUV with controls is calibrated in all three scenarios and has the power of the oracles: with 20 samples, estimating the confounders, the offset and the prior costs almost nothing. Without controls (sparsity), it is calibrated when most genes do not change, and fails, as it must, when most genes go up (§1.4). The standard pipelines are calibrated only without confounding; the higher power of CATE and of the spike-in pipeline comes with an FDP of 12–42 %.

Misspecified controls. The same confounded and global scenarios, but the 200 controls carry small effects: noisy controls, log₂ fold change $\sim \mathcal N(0, 0.2)$ each, or biased controls, which shift together, $\sim \mathcal N(0.15, 0.05)$, as a spike-in whose amount follows the condition.

Noisy controls are averaged out: PLNRUV with controls stays calibrated (2.6 % confounded, 4.1 % global). Biased controls are a different matter. In the confounded scenario every control-based method fails, PLNRUV included (FDP 54 %; DESeq2 with control size factors 56 %, the spike-in pipeline 53 %), because a shared shift of the controls moves the estimate of the offset, and with it every gene; the sparsity branch is not affected (4.8 %). In the global scenario PLNRUV with controls stays calibrated (1.4 %), with less power (0.56 against 0.69). Controls that move with the condition, even slightly, are the main threat to a control-based analysis (§8).

With few samples the picture changes: with 2–3 samples per group, the inference has only 1–2 residual degrees of freedom per gene and every method, the oracles included, has an excess of false discoveries (§8).
