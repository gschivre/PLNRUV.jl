8. Assumptions and limits

assumptionwherewhat would break it, and how to check
counts are Poisson given the latent abundance§2.1technical overdispersion beyond lognormal (PCR duplicates); multi-mapping reads give non-integer counts, which must be rounded
latent noise is Gaussian with one variance per gene§2.1outlier samples; heavy-tailed residuals
the hidden factors act linearly on the log scale, with $q$ factors§2.2choose $q$ with choose_q (§3.2); too few factors leave confounding, too many cost power and, with few samples, fit the noise
negative controls are truly null§2.3a shift of the controls moves every estimate: controls that shift together by 0.15 log₂ give an FDP of 54 % in a confounded design (§7.4); compare with the sparsity branch, and test with ctrl_signal in the simulator
or: most effects are zero and the effect distribution is unimodal at zero§4.1global changes of transcription (most genes up); then controls (spike-ins) are required
one covariate of interest at a time§3.1for several contrasts, run once per covariate; a joint (mash) prior is planned
the variational posterior is Gaussian and independent across cells§5.1its uncertainty is not used for the standard errors, which come from the model (§5.4)
the estimated loadings $\Gamma$ and confounders $\alpha$ are plugged in (empirical Bayes branch)§4.2slight excess of false discoveries with weak factors and low counts, and with very few residual degrees of freedom (2–3 samples per group); let choose_q choose fewer factors

Some questions cannot be answered from the reads alone, whatever the model:

  • a change common to all genes (total transcription) is invisible without spike-ins or controls (§1.4);
  • a hidden factor perfectly aligned with the design cannot be told apart from the effect: the controls, or sparsity, decide how it is split, and they can be wrong.

Very few samples. With $q$ chosen by choose_q, PLNRUV with controls is calibrated on thinned GTEx data from five samples per group (FDP 1.9 % without and 3.7 % with confounding), but not below: 16–22 % with three samples per group and about 66 % with two, where every method we tried fails as well. Two causes add up. With two or three samples per group the inference has one or two residual degrees of freedom per gene, so each gene's variance rests almost entirely on the shrinkage prior; on data simulated exactly from the model this alone gives null $z$-scores with tails about twice too heavy. And real samples differ in their overall variability (individuals, sample quality), which the model does not describe. Shrinking the variances towards their trend in the mean (var_trend = true, cubic or lowess) corrects the tails on simulated data, but on GTEx it makes the FDP worse (14 % instead of 2 % with five samples per group), because real genes scatter around the trend: it is not recommended as a fix. With fewer than five samples per group, treat the discoveries as candidates to confirm.
