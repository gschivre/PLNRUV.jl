PLNRUV — Module
PLNRUV

Poisson-lognormal differential expression with confounder adjustment, combining CATE of Wang J. et al., Ann. Stat. (2017): https://doi.org/10.1214/16-AOS1511 and the MOUTHWASH generalisation of Gerard D. & Stephens M. The compositional offset of sequencing data is a latent factor with loadings fixed to one.

PLNRUV.thin_2group — Function
thin_2group([rng,] counts; ngenes, nsamp, nctrl = 0, prop_null = 0.9, signal = Normal(0, 0.8), ctrl_signal = Dirac(0), group_prop = 0.5, target_cor = Float64[])

simulate a two-group differential expression data set from real counts (genes in rows, samples in columns) ngenes genes and nsamp samples are drawn at random, nctrl genes are negative controls, a proportion propnull of the other genes are null and the log2 fold changes of the rest are drawn from signal ctrlsignal allows controls that are only approximately null (e.g. Normal(0, 0.1)) targetcor sets the correlation between the group indicator and the first surrogate variables (confounding): with assignment = :rerandomise (default) the realised correlations are within margin of it (rerandomisegroups; when no assignment of a sample draw reaches it, new samples are drawn, at most maxdraws times), with :threshold they match it only on average (assigngroups); an empty targetcor gives random groups; the realised correlations with nrecord surrogate variables (at least length(target_cor)) are returned in all cases; the assignment uses its own random stream, so data sets that differ only by the target do not share their assignment draws return the thinned counts with samples in rows and genes in columns (the orientation used by the models)

PLNRUV.assign_groups — Function
assign_groups(rng, U, target_cor; group_prop = 0.5)

assign samples to two groups so that the group indicator has a target correlation with the columns of U a latent w = U * r + noise is thresholded at its quantile, and for a Gaussian w cor(1{w > t}, U[:, k]) = r[k] * φ(t) / sqrt(p * (1 - p)) with t the (1 - p) quantile

source
PLNRUV.rerandomise_groups — Function
rerandomise_groups(rng, U, target_cor; group_prop = 0.5, margin = 0.15, maxtries = 1000)

assign samples to two groups at random, and keep the assignment only if its realised correlations with the columns of U are within margin of the target: maxₗ ||cor(x, Uₗ)| - |targetcorₗ|| ≤ margin (the sign of a factor is arbitrary); rerandomisation (Morgan & Rubin 2012): first maxtries uniform random assignments (the assignment is then uniform among those that meet the target), then, when none met it, maxtries proposals of assigngroups, which aims at the target on average (with many samples a uniform split almost never reaches a strong target: the chance correlations have a standard deviation of about 1 / √n) return (x, tries), or nothing when no assignment met it in 2 maxtries draws (with few samples the possible correlations are few, and a target may be out of reach)

Empirical Bayes

PLNRUV.pln_mouthwash — Function
pln_mouthwash(Y, X, q; controls = Int[], cov_of_interest = size(X, 2), offset_factor = true, nullweight = 10, estimate_λ = true, likelihood = :t, mode = :auto, kwargs...)

empirical Bayes branch: PLN fit (stage 1) followed by MOUTHWASH (stage 2) bⱼ = u'Mⱼ / Rₖₖ with u the column of Q for the covariate of interest; its standard error uses the latent-scale variance of each count σⱼ² + 1 / ȳᵢⱼ (lognormal variance + Poisson noise, ȳᵢⱼ the expected count), the variances of M itself are too small in the residual rows that are shrunk toward the fitted factors with controls, mode = :auto centres the non-null part of the effect prior at an estimated mode when a shift shared by many genes (a global change of transcription) raises the held-out likelihood (see ash); the null stays at zero the PLN layer is fitted by plnpca (kwargs go to it); the posterior covariance of its loadings is passed to MOUTHWASH, which adds the error of the confounding correction, α'Γcovⱼα, to the noise of each gene

PLNRUV.plnpca — Function
plnpca(Y, X, q; offsets = log library sizes, offset_factor = true, τB = 5, var_prior = :trend, span = 0.2,
       maxiter = 1000, tol = 1e-7, prune = 1e-3)

Poisson-lognormal factor model fitted by variational empirical Bayes: counts Y (samples x genes), design X and q latent factors, with random factor scores in the residual space of X, random loadings, random coefficients (whose uncertainty gives the overdispersions a REML-type correction) and an inverse-gamma prior on the overdispersions, whose scale follows the mean log-abundance of the genes (varprior = :trend, a local fit over a fraction span of the genes; :common for a single scale, which shrinks the variances of weakly and of highly expressed genes towards the middle); every update is exact and the ELBO increases at every sweep; the loadings have one prior variance per factor (automatic relevance determination) and the hyperparameters are estimated from all genes; a factor whose prior variance falls below prune times the median variance is removed (q is an upper bound); maxiter counts sweeps, and the fit stops when the ELBO changes by less than tol per count (gene in a sample) return a PLNFit (σ2 = 1 / E[1 / σⱼ²], df = 2a0 + n - tⱼ as for a variance shrunk by limma, tⱼ ≈ k + q the degrees of freedom used by the coefficients and loadings of gene j, Γcov the posterior covariance of the loadings); the fit of plnmouthwash and choose_q on very sparse counts, or low counts with nearly equal variances (complete pooling), the variances approach their value slowly from above and the fit may stop at maxiter (converged = false), with overestimated, conservative variances

PLNRUV.choose_q — Function
choose_q(Y, X; qs = 0:6, ε = 0.5, nsplit = 2, split = :binomial, maxrounds = 5, offset_factor = true, rng, kwargs...)

number of latent factors of the PLN model by count splitting: Y is split in two (binomial thinning; split = :betabinomial for beta-binomial thinning with rⱼ = 1 / (exp(σⱼ²) - 1), which splits the latent noise as well as the Poisson noise), the model is fitted on the first part for each q in qs and scored by the predictive log-likelihood of the second part (averaged over nsplit splits); with the beta-binomial split σⱼ² comes from a fit at the current choice, from q = 0, repeated until the choice is stable (at most maxrounds); kwargs are passed to plnpca the automatic relevance determination of plnpca does not replace this choice: with a large q it keeps most factors (real data have more weak structure than noise), and each one costs residual degrees of freedom; but above the number of factors the data support, the chosen bound qmax no longer matters (the extra factors are switched off), and the scores of such bounds differ only by noise: q is the number of factors kept by the fit at qmax return q (factors kept), the chosen bound qmax, the scores of each bound in qs (last round) and the choices of the successive rounds

PLNRUV.ash — Function
ash(r, s; nullweight = 10, grid = ash_grid(r, s), df = Inf, mode = :auto, mode_threshold = 0.05)

ash for estimates r with standard errors s, nullweight is the Dirichlet weight of the point mass at zero the non-null part of the prior is unimodal at zero (mode = :zero) or around an estimated mode μ, the null staying at zero (mode = :estimate); mode = :auto estimates μ only when it raises the held-out log-likelihood by more than mode_threshold per estimate (a shift shared by many effects, such as a global change of transcription)

PLNRUV.evalues — Function
evalues(r, v, σ, π)

compound e-values of Ramdas A. & Wang R., Hypothesis testing with e-values (2024), Theorem 9.21 / Ignatiadis N., Wang R. & Ramdas A., arXiv:2409.19812 (2024): for rⱼ ~ N(βⱼ, vⱼ) and the prior Σₖ πₖ N(0, σₖ²) (σ₁ = 0 the point mass), the likelihood ratio of the non-null mixture to the null, eⱼ = qⱼ(rⱼ) / N(rⱼ; 0, vⱼ) with qⱼ = Σₖ₌₂ πₖ N(0, vⱼ + σₖ²) / (1 - π₁), equivalently eⱼ = π₁ / (1 - π₁) × (1 - lfdrⱼ) / lfdrⱼ; with an estimated prior they are asymptotically valid compound e-values

PLNRUV.ebh — Function
ebh(e; level = 0.05)

e-BH procedure of Wang R. & Ramdas A., J. R. Stat. Soc. B (2022): reject the k* hypotheses with the largest e-values, k* = max{k : k e₍ₖ₎ / K ≥ 1 / level}; controls the FDR under arbitrary dependence for compound e-values. Return a Boolean vector of discoveries
